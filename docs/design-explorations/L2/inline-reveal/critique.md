## Top 3 wins

1. **Reading flow as a feature.** Block → its discussion → next block, vertically. No lateral hop. The strip's flush, slightly-inset attachment with the elbow tick is the load-bearing visual idea and it works in the screenshot — the strips read as "of" their parent block, not adjacent to it.
2. **Honest "anchor lost" treatment.** Dashed hairline foot, mono caps `lost anchors (1)`, ghost surface, `--text-lo` chip — single indicator, no danger color, no two-state ambiguity. Exactly the concept's contract.
3. **Density without sacrificing affordance discoverability.** Persistent strips on already-anchored blocks mean the user sees discussion presence without hovering; only the *affordance* is hover-revealed. Variant's worst frame is "quiet dense prose," which is the right worst frame for a knowledge body.

## Top 3 issues

1. **Inline `discuss` pill breaks reading flow at button-in-paragraph junction.** Placing a pill-shaped, mono-caps button at the trailing edge of `<p>` content means: (a) screen readers read "…mark invoices paid. discuss button" in body context, (b) the pill's chrome (background + border + 2 px Y-pad) shifts baseline alignment by ~1 px against the cap-line, (c) when prose wraps, the pill can wrap onto a near-empty line, defeating "exactly where the eye finishes." Suggested fix: drop the chrome at rest (text-only `discuss` link in `--text-lo` mono-sm) on hover, OR move the pill to sit *outside* the `<p>` via a flex row whose first child is the paragraph and second child is the affordance. Keeps the trailing-edge intent without baseline compromise.
2. **Strip radius mismatch with parent.** Body frame uses `--r2` (8 px), code blocks use `--r2`, table uses `--r2` — but the strip is `--r1` (4 px). At flush attachment the inner corners of the strip don't echo the parent. Either move the strip to `--r2` or make the strip's *top* corners square (radius 0) and the bottom corners `--r1` to read as a continuation tab rather than a separate chip.
3. **Per-kind affordance position (3 modes) inflates the testing/visual-regression surface.** The spec accepts this trade-off, but the mockup shows it concretely: `trailing-inline` for paragraph/heading/list-item/quote, `chrome-top-right` for code/table, `image-overlay` for image. Three positions × two states (rest/hover/focus) × Lucid Dark + Light = 18 visual permutations to lock in golden screenshots. Worth flagging as a non-trivial DS-quality cost vs. Variant A's single uniform position.

## Verdict

**Recommend.** Score 8.3. This is the variant that takes the brief seriously — "density-first, no margin tax" — and delivers it with a single load-bearing visual idea (flush attached strip with elbow tick) that nothing else in the system has. The inline pill needs a chrome rethink before implementation, and the strip radius needs to echo the parent, but neither is structural — both are finishing fixes. The variant is honest about its weaknesses (discoverability, heavy-discussion bodies look chopped, sticky code chrome on mobile) and its concept-contract adherence (closed kind set, no dialect, no visible block ids, single lost-anchor indicator) is clean.
